# Rank first, then let GRPO fine-tune

GRPO should not drive this strategy, but it can be the last stage. A £10,000 account cannot move prices, so the weekly problem is a contextual bandit with full counterfactual feedback, coupled to a known, deterministic holdings state. The realised return vector scores every possible portfolio, so a pathwise gradient through a differentiable portfolio optimises the same in-sample objective that GRPO estimates by sampling, but without the sampling noise. That noise grows roughly as N/G, giving a noise-to-signal variance ratio of about 190 for 3,000 names and 16 samples. The published record points the same way. About ten GRPO trading papers exist, all but one of them preprints, and none studies a weekly long-only top-K book drawn from a large universe. The best-designed one beats equal weight by a Sharpe margin of about one standard error. Critic-free REINFORCE portfolios, and the one public EIIE replication on stocks, collapse to 1/N. The design that still honours the request has three stages. A small transformer that treats each stock as a token (about 150k parameters) is pretrained on a listwise/IC loss. It is then fine-tuned end-to-end on net-of-cost log utility. Only then does GRPO fine-tune it, in the no-std, on-policy, exact-KL form that is really RLOO. That last stage does one job differentiable training cannot: it learns the hold-or-swap decision under Trading 212's exact, asymmetric costs (about 0.30% round trip for US and most European names, 0.50% for UK, 0.70% for in-scope French names, before spread). Adoption has to be judged on net active return against the equal-weight universe, because the incumbent's Sharpe (1.18) equals that benchmark's (1.19). Its active-return t-stat is about 1.6, its weekly IC t-stat of 2.86 falls short of a multiple-testing hurdle, and the 2024–2026 holdout has been looked at for 88 variants. The realistic outcomes are therefore "blend" or "reject". The existing pool is a hand-picked list of 291 of today's developed-market large caps, 247 of them tradable on Trading 212, not an index. Widening it to a survivorship-free, rule-based universe of about 800–1,200 Trading 212-tradable mid and large caps is feasible and probably worth more than GRPO. **Most urgently: Trading 212's terms prohibit algorithmic trading with "limited to no human intervention". Neither this model nor the current one may run unattended against the live ISA.**

## Trading 212's terms ban the unattended version of this system

**Trading 212 expressly prohibits "Algorithmic Trading"** in its Invest Terms (cl. 5.1, updated 7 Aug 2026, which also govern the Stocks ISA) and in its API Terms (cl. 4.2(a), updated 17 Oct 2025). Both use the same definition: "any kind of trading in Instruments where a computer algorithm automatically determines individual parameters of Orders, such as whether to initiate the Order, the timing of execution, price or quantity of the Order, or how to manage the Order after its submission, with limited to no human intervention" ([T212 API Terms](https://www.trading212.com/legal-documentation/API-Terms_EN.pdf); [T212 Invest Terms](https://www.trading212.com/legal-documentation/uk/invest/Invest-Terms_EN.pdf)). Trading 212 reserves the right to close accounts that breach the ban. It can also suspend API access or change its limits at any time without notice (cl. 9.1). The API licence covers personal use "only for testing purposes" (cl. 6.1–6.2). "High-speed or automated mass data entry" and any "customised interface" need Trading 212's prior written consent (cl. 6.6–6.7). The API is still a beta: order POSTs are not idempotent, orders can be sized only by quantity, and no real-time market data comes with it (cl. 7.1(b)) ([T212 API docs](https://docs.trading212.com/api); [OpenAPI spec](https://docs.trading212.com/_bundle/api.json?download)).

This affects the current system as much as any GRPO challenger, because the definition is about who decides an order's parameters, not how the model was trained. The Saturday cloud job that runs `python -m wq t212 --execute` decides what, when and how much to trade, and submits it without a person in the loop ([PLAN.md §15](../PLAN.md)). Against the Practice server, with virtual money, it is plausibly the "testing" use the licence describes. **Pointed at `live.trading212.com` and the real ISA, the same job matches the prohibited definition word for word.** The live plan in PLAN.md is already compliant in spirit. Orders are "placed by hand on Monday" from an order sheet, and "you approve every trade" (§1, §9). That has to stay the operating model for any version of the strategy: the model writes the order sheet, and a human reviews it and either places the orders by hand or explicitly approves a batch that a script then submits, with each approval logged. Trading 212 publishes no threshold for what counts as enough human intervention, so the user should ask Trading 212 support in writing whether approve-then-submit qualifies, and treat silence as a no. The incumbent makes about 20 stock swaps a year ([PLAN.md §13](../PLAN.md)), so manual execution costs little time. A challenger that trades more raises that burden as well as its costs. That is one more reason to make low turnover a design goal rather than an afterthought.

The first Practice week already showed the mechanics a simulator must reproduce. Trading 212 rejected 8 of the 15 orders because UK and European instruments accept only 2–3 decimal places of quantity. A UBS buy was refused for insufficient free cash, because Trading 212 reserved about 5% extra for weekend-queued market orders (£9,580.81 blocked for 14 orders) ([orders_placed.csv](../paper/t212_practice/orders_placed.csv); repo commits `60af684`, `0609755`). This matches the Invest Terms. Buys block "a reasonable percentage above the current market price", an order may not exceed 95% of available cash, and orders sent while the market is closed are queued for the next open (cl. 12.4–12.7) ([T212 Invest Terms](https://www.trading212.com/legal-documentation/uk/invest/Invest-Terms_EN.pdf)). The environment used for training and backtesting should therefore round quantities to each instrument's precision, model the cash reservation, and enforce sells first, buys after fills. An RL policy trained without these frictions learns trades that cannot be placed.

## A £10k price-taker turns RL into a full-information bandit

### The weekly problem has no hidden dynamics

Write the market as features X_t for the N_t eligible stocks plus a market vector m_t, and the account as drifted holdings h_t. Market features evolve independently of the account's actions, and holdings evolve deterministically:

```
X_{t+1} ~ P(· | X_t)                                   (independent of the action w_t)
h_{t+1} = w_t ⊙ (1 + R_{t+1}) / (1 + w_tᵀ R_{t+1})      (known, differentiable a.e.)
J(θ)    = Σ_t [ log(1 − C(h_t → w_t)) + log(1 + w_tᵀ R_{t+1}) ]
```

With zero costs, J separates into independent weekly problems. Each week, R_{t+1} reveals the payoff of every possible portfolio, so there is no exploration–exploitation trade-off in the bandit sense. The EIIE authors made this point in 2017: under zero market impact the immediate reward "can be regarded as the action-value function of action w_t with a discounted factor of 0", which "justifies the full-exploitation approach" ([Jiang et al., arXiv 1706.10059](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10059)). With costs, today's choice changes next week's cost through h. But h's dynamics are known, so one can backpropagate through a multi-week unrolled backtest with no learned critic. **A stochastic policy is purely an optimisation device here, not a way to learn about an unknown environment.**

### What each part of GRPO does in this setting

GRPO is PPO without a critic. For each prompt it samples G outputs and uses the group mean as the baseline, with DeepSeekMath also dividing by the group's standard deviation. It keeps PPO's clipped importance ratio and adds a k3-estimator KL penalty to a frozen reference ([DeepSeekMath, arXiv 2402.03300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300)). Mapped onto a portfolio, most of that machinery has nothing to do. The critic it removes was never needed, because the one-step value is known exactly. Clipping does nothing on-policy: with a single gradient step per batch the clipped estimator reduces to the vanilla policy gradient ([Bereket & Leskovec, arXiv 2508.11800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11800)). Dr. GRPO's length-bias fix is irrelevant to a fixed-size action ([Liu et al., arXiv 2503.20783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20783)). The k3 "KL as loss" is a biased first-order approximation ([arXiv 2510.01555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01555)), and a Plackett–Luce policy allows the exact KL anyway.

The std normalisation is actively harmful. When every group member is scored against the same stochastic outcome, which is exactly the situation of one realised market week, std-normalised GRPO produces overconfident policies: expected calibration error was **0.239 for GRPO vs 0.002 without std normalisation** on synthetic Bernoulli tasks ([arXiv 2508.11800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11800)). In weekly terms, dividing by the per-week spread of group rewards down-weights high-dispersion weeks (crises, earnings seasons), where much of the real P&L variation lives. The spread also shrinks as the policy concentrates, so the effective step size grows and pushes the policy toward collapse. Take all of that out and what remains is REINFORCE with a leave-one-out baseline and a KL anchor, which is RLOO ([Ahmadian et al., arXiv 2402.14740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14740)). The design below calls it GRPO because that is the request, but the honest name is group-baseline REINFORCE.

The group-relative advantage does have one useful property here. If every sampled portfolio is fully invested, then 1ᵀw_i = 1ᵀw̄ = 1, and the advantage is exactly the return of a zero-net long–short portfolio:

```
A_i = r_i − r̄ = (w_i − w̄)ᵀ R_{t+1} − (c_i − c̄)      and   (w_i − w̄)ᵀ(R + m·1) = (w_i − w̄)ᵀ R
```

The week's common market move cancels exactly, which is what Trade-R1 calls removing "the non-stationary market trend (β)" ([arXiv 2601.03948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03948)). Differences in beta, sector and country exposure between samples still leak factor returns into the advantage. As a result, the policy is rewarded for tilting toward whatever factors happened to pay off in the training sample, such as a persistent US-tech tilt over 2016–2024. A cash sleeve reintroduces the market directly, which is one reason the design below keeps the book fully invested.

### Sampling noise and one-path overfitting

For a Gaussian-perturbed policy over N names with a locally linear reward, the score-function estimator has total variance of about (N+1)‖g‖², while the pathwise estimator has essentially none. A group of G samples therefore carries a relative noise² of roughly N/G just to approximate one week's exact gradient: **about 190 for N = 3,000 and G = 16, and still about 12 at G = 256**. A top-K Plackett–Luce policy has a comparable effective dimension. This is an analytical result from the project's [GRPO notes](../research_notes/GRPO%20transformer%20portfolio%20design/grpo_for_portfolios.md), building on the gradient-estimator taxonomy of [Mohamed et al., arXiv 1906.10652](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10652); it has not been tested empirically. On the current 245-name pool with G = 128 the ratio is about 2. That is one reason the GRPO stage is workable on today's pool but needs a candidate-set restriction on a 1,000–3,000-name universe.

A larger G does not protect against the deeper problem. Without std normalisation, the expected GRPO gradient is the gradient of the realised-path objective, the same one a direct method optimises. More samples only make the optimiser better at finding, for each historical week, the portfolios that did best that week. The KL-regularised optimum is the exponential tilt

```
π*(a | s_t) ∝ π_ref(a | s_t) · exp( r(a; R_{t+1}) / β )
```

which, for small β, literally means "buy what went up". A high-capacity transformer that can recognise a particular week from its features will memorise it. The number of independent outcome draws, about 870 weeks since 2010 and perhaps 4–6 market regimes, limits what RL can learn whatever the group size.

Positive same-data results compare RL with estimated-mean MVO, a weak baseline.

### Where GRPO still earns its place

Sampling-based policy gradients add value only where the objective is genuinely non-differentiable, and this strategy has exactly such an object: the live portfolio constructor. Hard top-15 equal weights, a country cap of 6, a £75 minimum trade, a 30% drift band, per-instrument quantity precision, and a cost schedule that charges UK and French buys but barely touches US sells together form a piecewise-constant map from scores to trades. The incumbent handles this map with hand-set knobs (a top-150 hold band, 4-week score smoothing), and these knobs cut costs from 7.7% a year to 0.8% ([PLAN.md §13](../PLAN.md)). GRPO can learn a holdings- and cost-aware hold-or-swap rule *through the exact constructor*, which differentiable relaxations only approximate. Two secondary uses also fit. GRPO can optimise path objectives (drawdown, Sortino) over H-week trajectories, using per-time-index group normalisation as in GR2PO ([arXiv 2609.19850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19850)). And if PLAN.md's Phase 4 adds an LLM that reads filings, GRPO is that model's native fine-tuner, provided it is tested only on data after the model's knowledge cutoff. Expect GRPO's gains, if there are any, to look like FinRankGRPO's: small, and inside sampling error unless the test is long.

## The formulation: holdings-aware scores, masked Plackett–Luce, exact net reward

### State, action and policy

The state each Friday is the token set of eligible stocks plus account context. Each token carries the stock's rank-normalised features x_i, a short weekly history, country/sector/currency type embeddings, and four account features: a held flag, the current drifted weight h_i, weeks held, and the stock's κ_i^buy and κ_i^sell. A date-level vector m_t (index returns and volatility, cross-sectional dispersion, rates) conditions the network. The action is the set S_t of K = 15 names to hold next week at equal weight, (1 − 0.02)/K each, which keeps the incumbent's 2% cash buffer and so the like-for-like comparison. The deterministic £75 minimum-trade and 30% drift rules then turn S_t into orders. These rules are treated as part of the environment, so the reward sees exactly what would be traded. Phase 1b found that 10–25 names and equal vs inverse-volatility weighting "make little difference" ([PLAN.md §14](../PLAN.md)), so K stays fixed and weighting stays equal. The learning problem is selection plus the hold decision.

The policy is a **masked sequential Plackett–Luce (Gumbel-top-K) sampler** over the transformer's scores s_i = f_θ(X_t, m_t, h_t, κ_t)_i. At step k it draws one name from a softmax over the names still allowed: not yet chosen, and from a country that has fewer than 6 picks. Constraints are thus enforced by masking, and the log-probability stays exact:

```
A_k = { j ∉ {i_1..i_{k−1}} : count_country(j) < 6 }
log π_θ(a | s_t) = Σ_{k=1}^{K} [ s_{i_k}/τ − log Σ_{j ∈ A_k} exp(s_j/τ) ]
```

This costs O(NK), and only K = 15 terms enter the log-probability, rather than N as with a Dirichlet or logistic-normal policy. Dirichlet policies are a poor fit here. Their samples are dense, sparse-looking draws need α_i ≪ 1, which makes log w_i numerically unstable, and the joint log-ratio sums over N terms. The deployed action is the greedy masked top-K under the same masks (τ → 0). Validation always scores this deterministic policy, never the stochastic one, because with costs the two differ. Because held names see their own h_i and κ^sell, the network can learn an incumbency bonus approximately equal to the switching cost. In other words, it learns its own hold band. This rule is what GRPO is asked to improve on, and it is compared against the incumbent's hand-tuned top-150 band.

### Reward

The per-week reward is net log growth minus an ex-ante risk charge:

```
r_t(a) = log(1 − C(h_t → w(a))) + log(1 + w(a)ᵀ R_{t+1}) − (γ/2) · w(a)ᵀ Σ̂_t w(a)
```

R_{t+1} is the GBP return from Monday's close to the next Monday's close, the same label timing as the incumbent ([PLAN.md §6](../PLAN.md)), so FX moves are part of what is learned. Σ̂_t is an ex-ante weekly covariance known at t, for example a country/sector/market-beta factor model plus idiosyncratic variance from trailing 104-week returns. Log growth is the Kelly objective, and it is the natural additive per-step reward ([EIIE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10059); [Lu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07694)). An ex-ante penalty is preferred over a realised-variance penalty because, within a group, realised variance mostly measures the shared market move, which is noise for this purpose. The risk term matters for a second reason. Because the group advantage cancels the market, absolute portfolio risk otherwise gets no gradient at all. γ should be calibrated rather than guessed. A γ of 4 at 18% volatility charges about 6.5% a year, comparable to the equity premium, so choose γ on inner validation to hit the risk target below. A myopic one-step reward charges today's cost but gives no credit for the future costs that holding saves, so the design also has an H-week variant. In it, each group member rolls its own policy forward from the same h_t for H = 8 weeks. The advantage at each time index is the member's reward-to-go minus the leave-one-out mean of the other members' reward-to-go at that index, following GR2PO's per-time-index normalisation ([arXiv 2609.19850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19850)).

### Risk controls for "modest risk"

The incumbent is not modest-risk today. It runs at **20.2% volatility against 13.9% for VWRL**, with a −24.7% maximum drawdown, and in the holdout it "took more risk" for a Sharpe of 1.00 against VWRL's 1.23 ([PLAN.md §13](../PLAN.md)). Modest risk has to be a pre-registered budget, not an aspiration. I propose ex-ante annualised volatility ≤ 16%, ex-ante beta to the equal-weight universe ≤ 1.1, at most 6 names per country (about 40% of the book), and per-name weight fixed at 1/K ≈ 6.5%. The 16% figure is my proposal, chosen to sit between the equal-weight universe's 14.5% and the incumbent's 20.2%. The first two are enforced through γ, plus a rejection mask during sampling for portfolios whose ex-ante vol exceeds the cap. The last two are enforced structurally. A sector cap of about 5 per 15 is a sensible risk option, accepting that a cap of 4 cost about 3 points of CAGR in a survivorship-biased backtest that rewards concentration in later winners ([PLAN.md §13](../PLAN.md)). There should be no learned market timing or cash sleeve. The regime filter cost about 3.4% a year by missing rebounds, and a cash action brings the market back into the group advantage. With perhaps 4–6 regimes in the data, timing cannot be learned credibly. The existing kill switches stay in force (20% drawdown, 12 weeks of negative live IC, a 2σ shortfall against the backtest over 6 months). Two operational guards are added on the order sheet: weekly turnover above 30% of the book needs explicit human sign-off, and every order shows its price deviation from the reference.

## A small stocks-as-tokens transformer, trained in three stages

### Architecture: attention across stocks, not across time

The evidence on transformers for equity ranking is consistent. Attention across stocks on a given date adds value; temporal self-attention over noisy price histories does not. In US monthly data, a transformer placed inside the stochastic discount factor reached an out-of-sample Sharpe of **4.6 against 3.9** for an otherwise identical attention-free network. The gain was largest among mega and large caps, but the result is gross, long-short and from a working paper ([Kelly et al., NBER w33351](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33351)). MASTER's alternating intra-stock and inter-stock attention reached an IC of **0.064 against 0.051 for XGBoost** on CSI300 ([arXiv 2312.15235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15235)). In Qlib's CSI300 benchmark, a vanilla temporal Transformer earned 2.73% a year (IR 0.40) against LightGBM's 9.01% (IR 1.02) on the same engineered features ([Qlib benchmarks](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib/blob/main/examples/benchmarks/README.md)). Trees remain state of the art on medium-sized tabular data ([Grinsztajn et al., arXiv 2207.08815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815)). On the same 15 price/volume features, the transformer is therefore unlikely to beat LightGBM by much on its own. PLAN.md reached the same conclusion: more tuning of those features "is unlikely to help" ([PLAN.md §14](../PLAN.md)). Its real advantages are that it takes holdings and costs as inputs, emits a policy directly, and blends well. Forecast combination is one of the most reliable gains in international ML return prediction ([Cakici et al., JEDC 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jedc.2023.104725)).

The concrete network is permutation-equivariant. Stocks are tokens with no stock-ID and no positional embedding on the stock axis, and a key-padding mask lets one set of weights handle any N_t, including listings and delistings ([Set Transformer, arXiv 1810.00825](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00825)). Each token embedding concatenates four things: the rank features mapped to [−0.5, 0.5] with missing values set to 0 plus a missing flag (the AIPM convention); a small temporal encoder, either a 1-layer GRU or an MLP over 26 weekly returns and volume changes, giving 32 dims; 4–8-dim country, sector and currency embeddings; and the four account features. Two to three pre-LayerNorm self-attention blocks across the stock tokens follow. A FiLM gate driven by m_t sits on the input, MASTER's market-guided gating. A linear score head outputs s_i, and an auxiliary head predicts next-week volatility; risk-aware auxiliary tasks reduced seed variance in DeepScalper ([arXiv 2201.09058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09058)). Compute is trivial. A 3,000-token week with d = 64 and 4 heads needs about 36M attention scores per layer, and a 245-token week runs comfortably on a CPU.

### Stage 1: supervised listwise pretraining (the reference policy)

Train on each week's full cross-section with an IC loss plus a ListNet term on cross-sectionally z-scored next-week GBP returns y:

```
L_sup = − corr_t(s, z(y)) − α · Σ_i softmax(z(y)/T_y)_i · log softmax(s)_i ,   α ≈ 0.5
```

In QuantBench's US loss comparison, IC loss gave the best returns, MSE was far worse, and naive pairwise ranking was negative ([arXiv 2504.18600](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18600v1)). The ListNet term trains the scores as Plackett–Luce logits (ListNet's top-one softmax is the first Plackett–Luce step), so the frozen Stage-1 network *is* π_ref for Stage 3 once its temperature τ_0 is fitted on inner validation. Early stopping uses rank IC on an inner validation block: the last 52–104 weeks of each training window, after a purge equal to the label horizon and a 13-week embargo. A 260-week rolling window over 245 names gives only about 64k stock-weeks per fit, which is small for a neural network. Pre-register one window policy for *both* arms. The options are rolling 260 weeks to match the live incumbent, or expanding from 2010, which roughly triples the late-sample data, with the incumbent re-run under the same choice. Retrain every 4 weeks; in QuantBench, 3-month retraining beat both slower retraining and none.

### Stage 2: deterministic end-to-end fine-tuning (the bar GRPO must clear)

Before any sampling, fine-tune the same network by backpropagating the reward above through a relaxed top-K portfolio. Unroll H = 8–13 weeks so that holdings drift and costs are charged against the previous week's soft weights:

```
θ_K = ½ (s_(K) + s_(K+1))   (stop-gradient)
w_i = (1 − b) · σ((s_i − θ_K)/τ_w) / Σ_j σ((s_j − θ_K)/τ_w) ,   τ_w annealed toward 0
L_e2e = − Σ_{t..t+H} r_t(w)  +  λ_anchor · ‖s − s_ref‖²
```

This is the AlphaPortfolio / Zhang–Zohren–Roberts approach of gradient ascent on the portfolio objective itself ([AlphaPortfolio](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f160875/f160875.pdf); [Zhang et al., arXiv 2005.13665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13665)), and it has far lower variance than any sampling estimator. Its output, run through the *hard* constructor, is the Stage-2 policy. The Stage-3 policy must beat it out of sample on deterministic net results, or the GRPO stage is discarded.

### Stage 3: GRPO through the exact constructor

Initialise from Stage 2 and keep the Stage-1 network frozen as π_ref. For each training week, take the start holdings h_t from the deterministic policy's own historical rollout, so that states are on-policy. In half the episodes, randomly swap 0–3 holdings so the network sees varied incumbency. Then draw G masked Plackett–Luce samples, pass each through the exact constructor and cost function, and update:

```
A_{t,g} = r_{t,g} − (1/(G−1)) Σ_{g'≠g} r_{t,g'}            (leave-one-out; no per-week std)
Â_{t,g} = A_{t,g} / σ_run                                    (running std over all weeks)
L_GRPO  = − (1/|B|G) Σ_t Σ_g Â_{t,g} · log π_θ(a_{t,g}|s_t)
          + β · (1/|B|G) Σ_t Σ_g Σ_k KL( p_θ(·|prefix_k, A_k) ‖ p_ref(·|prefix_k, A_k) )
```

Updates are strictly on-policy, one gradient step per sampled batch, so the importance ratio is 1 and clipping is moot. The KL is the exact categorical KL over the remaining allowed names at each of the K selection steps, conditioned on the sampled prefix. This avoids the k3 bias. Weeks whose G rewards are all identical are dropped, as in DAPO's dynamic sampling ([arXiv 2503.14476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476)). Kool et al.'s unordered-set estimator is a lower-variance drop-in once the basic version works, because equal weights make only the set matter, not the order ([arXiv 2002.06043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06043)). Overfitting is monitored with the mean pairwise Jaccard overlap of the G sampled sets and the Plackett–Luce entropy: overlap rising toward 1 means collapse. Another check is the gap between last-checkpoint and validation-best-checkpoint out-of-sample results. On a universe of 1,000 or more names, restrict sampling to the reference policy's top ~150 names plus current holdings, which keeps N/G near 1.

## The 291-name pool is hand-picked, and widening it matters more than RL

**The existing pool is not a global large-cap index.** `universe/global_large_caps.csv` holds **291 names: 136 US, 36 UK, 24 FR, 21 DE, 12 JP, 10 CH, 10 CA, 8 NL, 7 SE, 7 HK, 6 ES, 6 IT, 5 DK and 3 FI** ([global_large_caps.csv](../universe/global_large_caps.csv)). It was hand-picked as today's well-known large caps that can be traded on Trading 212 or IBKR. No index or size rule was applied, which is why PLAN.md treats every backtest as survivorship-biased and judges skill only against the equal-weight universe ([PLAN.md §4](../PLAN.md)). The file flags only **247 names as Trading 212-tradable: the US, UK, French, German, Swiss, Dutch, Spanish and Italian lines**. The 44 Japanese, Canadian, Swedish, Hong Kong, Danish and Finnish names are marked IBKR-only, and `wq/data.py` filters them out under `broker: t212`. The roughly 250-stock pool the backtest actually ran on (245 names after history filters) is therefore a US/Europe mega- and large-cap list, 55% US by count, with no Japan, Canada, Nordics, Asia or emerging markets. That matches the Trading 212 research: no Nordic, Hong Kong or Dublin venue is offered, and ISA access to TSX and Tokyo is unconfirmed ([T212 market hours](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/360008818178-What-are-the-markets-opening-and-closing-times)). It also means PLAN.md's "Nordics/Canada via IBKR" (§1) has no ISA equivalent on Trading 212. For scale, MSCI World has **1,249 constituents** across 23 developed markets, and MSCI ACWI has **2,414**, covering about 85% of each market's free-float cap. The smallest developed-market constituent holds about GBP 1.75bn of float ([MSCI World GBP factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-world-index-gbp-net.pdf); [MSCI ACWI factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-acwi-net.pdf)). The pool is about a fifth of MSCI World by count, selected partly *because* its members won. PLAN.md flags this for semiconductors, which are in the universe "because they won" ([PLAN.md §13](../PLAN.md)).

The RL design can expand to global mid and large caps, and that is where it is most at home. A permutation-equivariant transformer handles any N_t. Attention over 1,000–3,000 tokens fits on one GPU. Breadth gives cross-stock attention more context and gives the IC-based adoption tests more power, since each week contributes more stocks to the cross-section. Three constraints set the practical size. First, tradability. Counting the iShares ACWI holdings in markets Trading 212 serves gives roughly 750 local lines (about 515 US, 66 UK, 54 France, 50 Germany, 25 Netherlands, 21 Spain and a few Belgian, Portuguese and Austrian names). Swiss and Italian lines and US-listed ADRs of Asian companies take the realistic Trading 212-tradable universe to **about 800–1,200 names, not 2,400** ([iShares ACWI holdings](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239600/ishares-msci-acwi-etf/latest-holdings.csv)). Second, data. Yahoo cannot support it: yfinance users hit HTTP 429 after about 950 of 7,000 tickers ([yfinance #2431](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/discussions/2431)), and a delisted symbol loses "ALL DATA", which makes any Yahoo history structurally survivorship-biased ([node-yahoo-finance2 docs](https://github.com/gadicc/node-yahoo-finance2/blob/devel/docs/modules/historical.md)). Third, cost. Mid caps carry wider spreads, a FTSE 250-type name costs about 0.6–0.9% all-in round trip, and the liquidity floor needs raising for Gettex and OTC lines.

The affordable route is a **rule-based, survivorship-free universe on EODHD**. EOD All-World costs $199 a year if names are ranked by traded value. ALL-IN-ONE costs $999.90 a year and adds shares-outstanding history for market-cap ranking from 2018 ([EODHD pricing](https://eodhd.com/pricing)). Delisted tickers keep their history up to the delisting date, but names delisted before 2018 have prices only ([EODHD delisted docs](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis/delisted-stock-companies-data-2)). That makes trailing median GBP traded value the only size proxy consistent across the full history. Each month, rank all active and delisted common stocks on the Trading 212 venues, per region, using only data known at that date. Keep the top ~85% of cumulative size, or a top-N per region. Apply MSCI-style buffers: an incumbent stays until it falls below 1.5 times the entry rank. Require trading on at least 90% of days, with a floor of about £5–15m in daily value. Collapse share classes to one line per issuer, and inner-join on ISIN with Trading 212's `/equity/metadata/instruments` ([MSCI GIMI methodology](https://www.msci.com/eqb/methodology/meth_docs/MSCI_Jul26_GIMIMethod_Summary.pdf)). Validate the rule each quarter against SEC N-PORT ETF holdings, available from October 2019 ([SEC N-PORT](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-n-port-data-sets)). Norgate US Platinum ($630 a year) is worth adding to cross-check the US side, about 65% of global weight, with true historical index membership ([Norgate](https://norgatedata.com/stockmarketpackages.php)). The data is small: about 15M daily rows for 3,000 names over 20 years. **Do this before the GRPO stage.** It is the change PLAN.md §14 already identified as the source of real improvement. Re-running the incumbent on the clean universe sets the honest baseline, and the survivorship gap it reveals will probably exceed any plausible GRPO gain. Published survivorship-bias estimates run from about 1.6 points a year for US equities over 1926–2001 ([LuxAlgo](https://luxalgo.com/blog/survivorship-bias-in-backtesting-explained)) to 4.9 points for Indian small caps over 2016–2025 ([arXiv 2603.19380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19380)). For a deep-value strategy, adding delisting returns cut mean returns from about 30.1% to 24.6% ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/08/dealing-with-delistings-a-critical-aspect-for-stock-selection-research/)).

## The incumbent's edge is not yet significant, so validation must protect it

### What the repo's own numbers say

The incumbent's absolute Sharpe passes every deflation test trivially because it is mostly market beta. **Its Sharpe of 1.18 equals the equal-weight universe's 1.19** ([PLAN.md §13](../PLAN.md)). Measured as active return against that benchmark across 11 calendar years (2026 partial), it earns **+7.8 points a year with a 15.9-point standard deviation: IR 0.49, t ≈ 1.6** ([by_year.csv](../reports/backtest/by_year.csv)). 2020 alone contributes 43.7 of the roughly 86 cumulative points, and without 2020 the mean falls to +4.2 points (t ≈ 1.2). Weekly IC is 0.0225 with t = 2.86 for the ML model and 2.59 for the traded blend ([summary.json](../reports/backtest/summary.json)). Harvey, Liu and Zhu argue a new factor should clear t > 3 ([NBER w20592](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)), and a one-sided Bonferroni hurdle for about 100 logged trials is 3.29. The ML model's Sharpe advantage of 0.37 over its own momentum baseline has a block-bootstrap 95% CI of [−0.06, +0.78] after 10.8 years ([weekly_ml.csv](../reports/backtest/weekly_ml.csv) vs [weekly_baseline.csv](../reports/backtest/weekly_baseline.csv), computed in the [validation notes](../research_notes/GRPO%20transformer%20portfolio%20design/validation_overfitting.md)). And the "untouched" July 2024 – October 2026 holdout has a `hold_sharpe` value for all **88 Phase-1b variants** ([phase1b_variants.csv](../docs/phase1b_variants.csv)), so it is no longer clean for any challenger. This cuts both ways. The bar for "the challenger is different" is low, but the bar for "the challenger is real" is high. The only clean evidence left is forward paper trading from 17 October 2026, held-out regions, and, once the universe is widened, names and periods never used in tuning.

Statistical power is the binding constraint. Using the Memmel variance, detecting a 0.3 Sharpe difference at 80% power between two long-only books whose weekly returns correlate at 0.8 takes **about 35 years** of weekly data, and about 9 years even at 0.95. Under fat tails the requirement is longer, which is why Ledoit and Wolf's studentized block bootstrap replaces Jobson–Korkie ([Ledoit & Wolf 2008](https://uzh.ch/cmsssl/econ/dam/jcr:ffffffff-935a-b0d6-0000-00007214c2bc/jef_2008pdf.pdf)). IC tests are more powerful because every week contributes a cross-section. With the incumbent's weekly IC SD of about 0.186 and an IC correlation of 0.7 between models, a ΔIC of 0.02 needs about 400 weeks and a ΔIC of 0.01 about 1,600. The adoption decision therefore rests on the backtest protocol and IC tests. Paper trading checks the implementation and enforces a kill rule; it cannot prove superiority.

### Protocol

Commit a pre-registration file and an append-only trial ledger before any challenger out-of-sample number is seen. The ledger records every architecture, reward shape, β, checkpoint rule and seed, because "the most important piece of information missing from virtually all backtest reports is the number of trials attempted" ([Bailey et al. FAQ](https://mathinvestor.org/2014/04/faqs-on-backtest-overfitting/)). The primary metric is the annualised IR of net weekly active return against the equal-weight universe. It is computed under one cost model and identical portfolio rules for every arm except the GRPO arm, whose learned hold rule is the thing under test. Validation has three layers. An outer walk-forward, re-fit every 4 weeks, produces the "tradable record". Combinatorial purged cross-validation (CPCV) with N = 10 groups and k = 2 (45 splits, 9 full paths, a 1-week purge and a 13-week embargo) gives a distribution of out-of-sample IRs rather than a single path. Nested inner validation handles every hyperparameter and early-stopping choice ([skfolio CPCV](https://skfolio.org/_modules/skfolio/model_selection/_combinatorial.html)). Every configuration runs 10 seeds. The incumbent is re-run with the same 10 seeds, so the probability-of-improvement statistic compares like with like. Results are reported as the interquartile mean (IQM) across seeds × strata, with stratified-bootstrap CIs, as rliable recommends ([Agarwal et al., arXiv 2108.13264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264v4); [rliable](https://github.com/google-research/rliable)). Before the main runs, a placebo battery must pass. Within-week label shuffles must show no skill. A planted signal at IC ≈ 0.02 must be recovered without being exceeded, since exceeding it indicates leakage. And the challenger is placed as a percentile in a distribution of 1,000 random-signal portfolios built under the same rules. On top of these come the DSR on active returns with N from the ledger ([fynance DSR](https://fynance.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/fynance.research.deflated_sharpe_ratio.html)) and PBO via CSCV with S = 16 ([Bailey et al., SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). The ablation ladder is the heart of the GRPO question. The arms are: (a) incumbent LightGBM blend; (b) Stage-1 transformer scores through the incumbent's rules; (c) Stage-2 direct fine-tune through the same rules; (d) Stage-3 GRPO with its learned hold rule; (e) the best of (b)–(d) blended 50/50 with (a). **Reject (d) if it fails to beat (c) on deterministic net IR**, whatever it does against (a).

### Decision rules

| Outcome | Conditions (all fixed before any out-of-sample run) |
|---|---|
| **Adopt** (replace incumbent) | DSR(active) ≥ 0.95; PBO ≤ 0.2; lower 95% bound of probability-of-improvement over incumbent seeds > 0.5; Ledoit–Wolf one-sided p < 0.05 or HAC ΔIC t > 2; positive active return in ≥ 3 of 4 sub-periods (2016–18, 2019–21, 2022–24, 2024–26) and in a held-out region; all placebos pass; still ahead at 2× costs; ex-ante risk budget met; ≥ 26 weeks of shadow paper trading with live IR inside the backtest's 90% bootstrap band |
| **Blend** (e.g. 50/50 score average) | Probability of improvement and placebos pass, active-return correlation with the incumbent < 0.6, but the superiority test fails; the blend's own DSR is added to the ledger and must pass |
| **Reject** | Anything else; keep LightGBM and record the negative result honestly |
| **Kill** (live or shadow) | Cumulative active return vs incumbent < −2·TE·√(weeks/52), or live IC t < −2 after 52 weeks, or any existing PLAN.md kill switch |

The thresholds are conventions and judgement calls, not derived optima ([validation notes §7](../research_notes/GRPO%20transformer%20portfolio%20design/validation_overfitting.md)). Given the power numbers above, "blend" or "reject" is the most likely result. The protocol's main job is to stop a lucky seed or checkpoint from replacing a working incumbent.

## Conclusion

The request treats GRPO as the learning engine, but the structure of a price-taking weekly book reduces GRPO to its leftovers. With no critic to remove, no clipping that matters on-policy, no variable length to normalise, and a std normalisation that should be dropped, it becomes leave-one-out REINFORCE with a KL anchor. What survives is still useful. GRPO is a principled way to optimise *through* the non-differentiable live portfolio constructor and Trading 212's asymmetric costs. That turns the incumbent's hand-tuned hold band, smoothing window and drift rule, the knobs that took costs from 7.7% to 0.8% a year, into something learned from holdings and costs. GRPO fits best as a narrowly scoped, KL-anchored fine-tuner of turnover decisions, and should be dropped the moment it fails to beat the deterministic direct fine-tune.

The deeper lesson is about where the remaining uncertainty lies. The incumbent cannot yet be distinguished from its own equal-weight universe at conventional significance, so a new model is unlikely to fail for lack of sophistication. It fails when it fits the one realised path of a hand-picked, survivorship-biased pool. That reorders the work: compliant execution and correct costs first, a clean and wider universe second, a transformer that blends with LightGBM third, and GRPO last, judged only on whether it improves the hold-or-swap decision net of real costs.
