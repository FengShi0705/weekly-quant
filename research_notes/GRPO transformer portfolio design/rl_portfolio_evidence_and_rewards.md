## Q1. Main deep-RL portfolio methods and benchmarks: what out-of-sample evidence they report (costs, universes, test lengths), and what has been independently replicated

### Takeaway
The best-known deep-RL portfolio results come from short tests (EIIE: three tests of about 50 days on 2016–17 crypto), long-short US/China equity books (AlphaStock, AlphaPortfolio, DeepTrader, HRPM), or the 30 Dow stocks over about one year (FinRL). Cost assumptions are often 0.1–0.25% or are not stated. Little has been independently replicated. The one public EIIE replication found that on stocks the learned policy collapses to equal weight, and a 2026 multi-seed re-evaluation found that headline gains often disappear.

### Cited Findings

**EIIE (Jiang, Xu & Liang 2017) [PP, arXiv q-fin.CP]**
- Framework components: Ensemble of Identical Independent Evaluators (CNN/RNN/LSTM variants), Portfolio-Vector Memory (PVM), Online Stochastic Batch Learning, and an explicit reward (average log return). Universe: Poloniex crypto, the 11 most-traded non-cash coins pre-selected by volume just before each experiment, with BTC as "cash". 30-minute trading periods. Commission of 0.25% on buys and sells (the Poloniex maximum). — [arXiv 1706.10059](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10059)
- Headline result: at least 4-fold returns in 50 days, from three back-tests of about 51 days each, with the 0.25% commission included. — [arXiv 1706.10059](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10059); [Wirz review](https://www.chriswirz.com/reviews/eiie-deep-portfolio-management/index.html)
- **Independent replication** (2024 master's thesis) [Thesis/PP]: the crypto results matched the original, but "when it comes to the stock market, the magic of this framework disappeared and fell back to be equivalent to the Uniformly Constant Rebalanced Portfolios algorithm or the equally weighted". The RL process "gradually makes our agent follow this equally weighted strategy". — [arXiv 2409.08426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08426)

**DeepTrader (Wang, Huang, Tu, Zhang, Xu, AAAI-21, vol. 35, pp. 643–650) [PR]**
- Market-condition embedding (a "market scoring unit") sets the split between long and short funds. Negative maximum drawdown is used as the reward for that unit. A GCN over an estimated causal structure models relations between assets, and the paper reports it captures them better than industry classification or correlation. Tested on "three well-known stock indexes" and reported superior on risk-return criteria. Code is public. — [AAAI OJS 16144](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16144); [GitHub CMACH508/DeepTrader](https://github.com/CMACH508/DeepTrader)
- It is a long-short design, so it does not match a long-only retail use case. Later papers use it as a baseline (for example MARS on HSI 2022), but no independent replication of its headline numbers was found. — [MARS arXiv 2508.01173](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01173v1)

**HRPM: "Commission Fee is not Enough" (Wang, Wei, An, Feng, Yao, AAAI-21, pp. 626–633) [PR]**
- Hierarchical RL. A high-level policy sets portfolio weights at low frequency, and a low-level policy executes buys and sells at higher frequency to reduce slippage, motivated by the point that earlier methods assumed instant execution and ignored price slippage. Reports significant improvement over state-of-the-art methods on US and China markets. — [AAAI OJS 16142](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16142); [arXiv 2012.12620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12620)

**AlphaStock (Wang, Zhang, Tang, Wu, Xiong, KDD 2019 Applied DS track) [PR]**
- Attention networks inside a Sharpe-ratio-oriented RL framework, with cross-asset attention. Builds a long-short "buy winners, sell losers" portfolio. Tested on "long-periodic" US and Chinese markets. The authors' interpretation is that it favours stocks with high long-term growth, low volatility, high intrinsic value and recent undervaluation. — [arXiv 1908.02646](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02646)

**AlphaPortfolio (Cong, Tang, Wang, Zhang) [WP: SSRN 3554486; NBER w35195]**
- Transformer-based RL trained end-to-end on the evaluation criterion (for example Sharpe), with no separate return forecast. US equities, monthly rebalancing. Out-of-sample "Sharpe ratio above two and risk-adjusted alpha over 13%", and this holds when small or illiquid stocks are excluded. Not yet in a peer-reviewed journal as of these sources. — [NBER w35195](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35195); [SSRN 3554486](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3554486)
- Cost sensitivity table in one version of the paper (row labels were garbled in the extract, so verify): all firms Sharpe 2.02 at 0.1% cost and 1.68 at 1% cost, turnover 0.20 and 0.16; firms above the 10th size percentile Sharpe 2.38 and 2.35, turnover 0.23 and 0.19. — [AlphaPortfolio PDF (AUT copy)](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/573128/AlphaPortfolio-updated.pdf); [EFMA 2021 version](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2021-Leeds/papers/EFMA%202021_stage-2049_question-Full%20Paper_id-342.pdf)

**DeepScalper (Sun, Xue, Wang, He, Zhu, Li, An, CIKM 2022, pp. 1858–1867) [PR]**
- Intraday trading, not portfolio allocation. Components: dueling Q-network with action branching, a "hindsight bonus" reward term for whole-day horizon, a multi-modal market encoder, and a risk-aware auxiliary task (volatility prediction). On stock-index data it beats the second-best method by 30.80% in total return, 33.33% in Sharpe, 21.42% in Calmar and 7.50% in Sortino, with p < 0.01 on 7 of 8 metrics across two datasets. The auxiliary task is reported to reduce performance variance across random seeds. — [arXiv 2201.09058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09058)

**FinRL / FinRL-Meta / FinRL Contests (Columbia / AI4Finance) [PR conference papers + PP]**
- FinRL ensemble paper (Yang, Liu, Zhong, Walid, ICAIF '20) [PR]: PPO, A2C and DDPG ensemble on the 30 Dow stocks. It beat each single agent and two baselines (DJIA and a min-variance portfolio) on Sharpe. — [ICAIF 2020 PDF](https://openfin.engineering.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/publications/ensemble.pdf)
- FinRL library reproduction on the Dow 30: daily data, training 2009-01-01 to 2020-06-30, **testing only 2020-07-01 to 2021-06-30 (one year, in a strong bull market)**. — [FinRL implementation write-up](https://sia.hackernoon.com/finrls-implementation-of-drl-algorithms-for-stock-trading)
- FinRL Contests 2023–2025 summary [PR, Wiley *AI for Engineering* 2025]: covers MDP task formulation, a data pipeline, GPU-parallel environments and standardised evaluation. Lists the obstacles as low signal-to-noise, survivorship bias of historical data and overfitting. States that "RL policies are unstable, whose performance is sensitive to hyperparameters, market noise and random seeds". Contestants were encouraged to use ensembles. 46, 21 and 85 teams entered in 2023, 2024 and 2025. — [Wiley AIE 2025](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/aie2.12004); [arXiv 2504.02281](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02281v2)
- Ensemble revisit of the 2023/24 contests [PP]: PPO, SAC and DDPG ensembles "outperform some individual agents", with up to 4.17% lower max drawdown and up to +0.21 Sharpe. GPU-parallel simulation gives up to 1,746× faster sampling with 2,048 environments. — [arXiv 2501.10709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10709)
- The 2025 contest added a FinRL-DeepSeek track that feeds LLM-derived news sentiment and risk scores into RL agents. No final out-of-sample leaderboards were found in the public docs. — [FinRL Contest 2025 docs](https://finrl-contest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/finrl2025/overview.html)
- FinRL-X (2026) [PP] moves to rolling out-of-sample validation with "all decisions at time t rely strictly on information available up to t", backtesting January 2018 to October 2025. — [arXiv 2603.21330](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21330)
- FinRL-Meta (NeurIPS 2022 Datasets & Benchmarks): market environments and benchmarks. — [arXiv 2211.03107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03107)

**TradeMaster (Sun et al., NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks) [PR]**
- An open-source RL-for-trading platform: toolkit, benchmark and UI. Evaluation uses PRUDEX-Compass (a separate TMLR paper) with five axes: profitability, risk control, universality, diversity and reliability. Metrics include Sharpe, volatility and max drawdown, plus performance profiles and rank distributions. The portfolio-management benchmark covers 8 RL algorithms on US stocks, with EIIE as a tutorial example. — [NeurIPS 2023 D&B](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/b8f6f7f2ba4137124ac976286eacb611-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html); [Appendix](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/b8f6f7f2ba4137124ac976286eacb611-Supplemental-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf); [TradeMaster EIIE tutorial](https://trademaster.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial/tutorial1.html)

**PPO/SAC/DDPG/A2C/TD3 portfolio agents (generic)**
- Controlled synthetic study, Lu (NUS) [PP, revised Aug 2025]. Correlated GBM plus Bertsimas–Lo market impact, with a Kelly (log-utility) objective so the optimal policy is known analytically. **DDPG, TD3 and SAC "were unable to learn the right Q function due to the noisy rewards"**. PPO and A2C with GAE reached a near-optimal policy, and PPO clipping helped keep it there. With regime switches, PPO plus an HMM learned regime-specific policies. **The simplest setting needed over 2 million steps, "equivalent to almost 8,000 years of daily prices"**, and the author concludes that sample complexity is too high for real data. — [arXiv 2307.07694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07694)
- Velay et al. (INISTA 2023) [PR conference] benchmarked classical DRL portfolio algorithms: "most DRL algorithms were not robust, with strategies generalizing poorly and degrading quickly during backtesting". — [arXiv 2306.10950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10950)
- Kashif & Ślepaczuk (May 2026) [PP, arXiv/SSRN]. SAC with transaction costs, turnover penalties and diversification constraints inside the reward. Flat versus hierarchical Dirichlet policy, LSTM versus Transformer encoder. Walk-forward test over **16 out-of-sample folds, 2003–2026, on Nasdaq-100, Nikkei 225 and Euro Stoxx 50**. **"No strategy achieves statistically significant excess returns relative to Buy and Hold under HAC-robust inference across all markets."** Euro Stoxx 50 shows significant abnormal returns. RL added most value in high-uncertainty periods, and ensembling across markets helped risk-adjusted performance. — [arXiv 2605.17307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17307); [SSRN 6780241](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6780241)
- Global Finance Journal 2024 [PR]: RTC-CNN-TD3 on DJIA stocks reached about 33.69% cumulative return by March 2023, against about 4–5% for max-Sharpe and min-variance baselines. Test length and costs were not visible in the extract. — [ScienceDirect S1044028324000887](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1044028324000887)
- Cost-sensitive PPN (Zhang, Zhao, Wu, Li, Huang, Tan) [PP; journal venue unconfirmed]: a two-stream policy network with a reward that maximises accumulated return while constraining transaction and risk costs. The authors claim a growth-rate optimality result and report beating EIIE. — [arXiv 2003.03051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03051)

**Multi-seed re-evaluation of established architectures [PR]**
- "Unstable Gains: Multiplicity-Aware Evaluation of Financial Deep RL" (2026; the ScienceDirect PII S2405-9188 corresponds to *The Journal of Finance and Data Science*). The authors re-run established DRL architectures over many seeds in equity trading and crypto portfolio allocation. Sharpe ratios "vary substantially across random seeds", apparent outperformance "often disappears under multi-run evaluation", and leaderboard gaps between algorithms "frequently lose statistical significance after permutation testing and family-wise error correction". — [ScienceDirect S2405918826000309](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405918826000309)

**GRPO-specific (relevant to this project folder)**
- FinRankGRPO (Sept 2026) [PP]: GRPO post-trains an LLM to produce a listwise ranking of assets, rewarded by Spearman rank correlation with realised orderings. The ranks then feed a mean-variance-style optimiser. Motivation: LLMs are poor at precise numerical forecasts. — [arXiv 2609.24175](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24175v1)
- GRPO itself (DeepSeekMath) drops PPO's learned critic and uses group-standardised rewards as the baseline. — [arXiv 2402.03300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300)

### Inferences
- **No headline result transfers directly to weekly, long-only, 10–20-name retail portfolios.** EIIE is 30-minute crypto. AlphaStock, AlphaPortfolio and DeepTrader are long-short and partly China. DeepScalper is intraday. FinRL tests one year on 30 names. The closest setup, SAC on large-cap global indices with costs and walk-forward folds (Kashif & Ślepaczuk 2026), finds no robust significant edge over buy-and-hold.
- The EIIE stock replication collapsing to equal weight fits the DeMiguel et al. "1/N" result: with a low-signal reward, the cost-minimising, diversification-maximising fixed point is equal weight. Any RL agent here should be benchmarked against equal weight and against the supervised ranking model's top-K at equal weight.
- AlphaPortfolio's turnover of about 0.16–0.23 (if read correctly as monthly) is far lower than a weekly top-K strategy typically trades. Its Sharpe of 2+ is long-short on the US cross-section including mid and small caps, so it is not evidence for a long-only, 15-name book.
- Lu's sample-complexity result scales badly to weekly data: 2 million steps is about 38,000 years of weekly bars for a single-path learner. Weekly RL is only viable if each date supplies many samples, for example cross-sectional or group samples as in GRPO, or if the problem is turned into a bandit or supervised problem.

### Gaps
- Exact DeepTrader, AlphaStock, HRPM, TradeMaster and PRUDEX-Compass tables (returns, Sharpe, test windows, assumed cost rates) were not retrievable. The full PDFs are needed.
- I could not confirm the FinRL ICAIF-2020 test window or cost (often quoted as 0.1%) from primary text.
- No independent replications were found for DeepTrader, AlphaStock, AlphaPortfolio, HRPM or DeepScalper. Apart from the "Unstable Gains" re-runs, I found no third-party re-implementation that reported matching numbers.
- No public, audited live-capital record for any of these methods was found.
- FinRankGRPO's empirical results, costs and test period were not visible.

## Q2. Surveys and critical reviews (2022–2026): conclusions on real-world performance, reproducibility and overfitting, and whether RL beats supervised ranking plus simple rules or mean-variance

### Takeaway
The 2023–2026 critical literature agrees on the main point. RL can look strong in single-seed backtests, but no AI architecture has been shown to deliver persistent net alpha after costs. Results are seed-sensitive, hyperparameter-sensitive and data-starved. Clean head-to-head tests of RL against a well-tuned supervised ranker with top-K equal weight on identical data, costs and splits are essentially absent. The few same-data comparisons against mean-variance or deep-learning forecasters favour RL but are small, single-study and workshop-level, and some are contradicted by robustness checks against minimum-variance or equal-weight baselines.

### Cited Findings

**Critical reviews and surveys**
- Zhu & Cai (2026) [PP, literature cutoff 31 Aug 2026]: "technical capability… is not evidence of investment profitability". Proposes an "alpha-translation chain": a stable prediction must become feasible positions, then executable orders, then risk-adjusted returns after costs. Lists failure points: temporal contamination, repeated selection, survivorship, weak benchmarks, implementation costs, venue mechanics and capacity. Notes "predictor decay, corrected look-ahead failures, mixed prospective evidence, and few audited live-capital records". Conclusion: "no general AI architecture is shown to deliver persistent, cross-regime, capacity-aware net alpha." — [arXiv 2609.04917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04917)
- Pippas et al., "The Evolution of RL in Quantitative Finance: A Survey" (ACM, 2025) [PR]: models must be carefully validated against overfitting; "excessively high Sharpe ratios call for scrutiny" because they can come from overfitting or data snooping, and a Sharpe above 3 is exceptional. Open problems: sim-to-real transfer, sample efficiency, evaluation practice, and over-engineering without matching gains. — [ACM DOI 10.1145/3733714](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3733714); [arXiv 2408.10932](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10932v2)
- Taxonomy plus experimental study of DRL in portfolio management (*Artificial Intelligence Review*, 2024) [PR]: stock-market data volume is often insufficient for DRL, "leading to low reproducibility of results", and performance depends heavily on hyperparameter tuning. — [Springer s10462-024-11066-w](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-024-11066-w)
- Hoque, Ferdaus & Hassan (Dec 2025) [PP]: systematic review of 167 articles from 2017–2025. Implementation quality and domain expertise matter more than algorithm choice, and hybrid architectures beat pure RL. The authors find the RL advantage strongest in market making. Some of the evidence they review is synthetic-data based. — [arXiv 2512.10913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10913)
- Hambly, Xu & Yang, "Recent Advances in RL in Finance" (Mathematical Finance 2023, per the brief; the arXiv v4 is dated Feb 2023): covers MDP, value-based and policy-based methods, with application sections on execution, portfolio optimisation, hedging, market making, order routing and robo-advising. — [arXiv 2112.04553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04553); [SSRN 3971071](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3971071)
- An RL-for-trading survey: buy-and-hold was the most common benchmark and not all methods beat it. Separately, studies "often fail to compare… against traditional methods used by financial professionals". — [arXiv 2212.06064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06064)
- Practitioner view (Man Group CIO, "AI Diary of a Quant"): ML has been part of AHL's process for more than a decade, and earlier ML techniques were rendered "useless in the face of low signal-to-noise ratios" by limits on compute and data. I found no Man, AQR or Two Sigma note that specifically evaluates RL for portfolio construction. — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/the-big-picture-ai)

**Same-data comparisons: RL against mean-variance or other models**
- Sood, Papasotiriou, Vaiciulis & Balch (J.P. Morgan AI Research), FinPlan'23 workshop at ICAPS 2023 [WS; arXiv 2602.17098]. Argues that a fair test needs RL and MVO to optimise the same objective. Uses PPO with a differential-Sharpe reward, averaging 5 seeds per year. Backtest 2012–2021: **Sharpe 1.17 for DRL against 0.68 for MVO**, return about 12.1% against 6.5%, lower max drawdown in "virtually every year", and less frequent rebalancing, which implies lower costs. — [ICAPS FinPlan23 PDF](https://icaps23.icaps-conference.org/papers/finplan/FinPlan23_paper_4.pdf); [arXiv 2602.17098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17098); [EmergentMind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2602.17098)
  - Robustness follow-up (Lund thesis) [Thesis]: against a **minimum-variance** portfolio, "DRL models did not clearly surpass MVO". — [Lund thesis](https://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/record/9178260/file/9178261.pdf)
- Ngo, Nguyen & Nguyen (2023), *Research in International Business and Finance* [PR]: on Vietnam (frontier) and US markets, the RL model had the highest return and Sharpe and "consistently outperforms conventional approaches". Deep-learning forecasters did well in favourable markets but lost their advantage in adverse ones, and traditional optimisation underperformed both by a wide margin. The authors acknowledge there is very little empirical evidence in this area. — [ScienceDirect S0275531923000624](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531923000624)
- Benchmark on 45 Nifty-50 stocks with 4 DRL models and 6 traditional strategies, run per market-efficiency cluster [venue unclear, ResearchGate]: DRL was best in highly efficient clusters, but in moderately and least efficient clusters "traditional strategies often achieve similar or better returns". — [ResearchGate 413152166](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/413152166_Benchmarking_deep_reinforcement_learning_and_classical_models_for_portfolio_optimization_across_market_efficiency_regimes)
- EIIE stock replication: performance only "average"; the policy converged to equal weight / UCRP. — [arXiv 2409.08426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08426)
- Kashif & Ślepaczuk 2026: no significant excess return over buy-and-hold across all three markets under HAC inference. — [arXiv 2605.17307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17307)

**Supervised and end-to-end alternatives (no RL)**
- Zhang, Zohren & Roberts, "Deep Learning for Portfolio Optimisation" [PP; arXiv 2005.13665]: an LSTM maps features to weights and is trained by gradient ascent on the Sharpe ratio directly, with no return forecast and no RL. Index ETFs, test period 2011 to April 2020; reported best among the compared methods, with cost-rate and volatility-scaling sensitivity studied. — [arXiv 2005.13665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13665)
- Extension, "A Universal End-to-End Approach to Portfolio Optimization via Deep Learning": optimises Sharpe, mean-variance or other objectives end to end. — [arXiv 2111.09170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09170)
- Learning-to-rank for cross-sectional strategies (Poh, Lim, Zohren, Roberts): LTR beats regression and heuristic benchmarks for cross-sectional momentum portfolios. — [arXiv 2012.07149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07149)
- Listwise LTR, long-short, China A-shares, 68 factors, 2006–2019: about 38% annual return out of sample, Sharpe about 2. — [arXiv 2104.12484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12484)
- An NDCG-style ranking objective for top-K US equity construction improved Sharpe over regression objectives. — [MDPI IJFS 14(4) 95](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/14/4/95)
- An explainability study comparing ML forecasters and DRL agents: the ML methods "rely on single-step prediction", while DRL agents "find portfolio weights with a long-term goal". The extract did not include the performance ranking. — [arXiv 2111.03995](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03995)

### Inferences
- **The literature does not show that RL beats a well-tuned supervised ranker plus a simple top-K rule on identical data.** I found no study running that comparison with the same universe, costs and walk-forward splits. Positive same-data results (Sood et al.; Ngo et al.) compare against MVO or forecasters fed into MVO, and estimated-mean MVO is a notoriously weak baseline. Against minimum-variance, equal weight or buy-and-hold, the RL advantage weakens or disappears (Lund thesis; EIIE replication; Kashif & Ślepaczuk).
- For a price-taking £10k account, actions do not move prices. The only path dependence is through current holdings, which drive transaction costs. The problem is therefore close to a sequence of contextual bandits coupled by a turnover cost, and realised net returns of any candidate portfolio can be computed directly from data. That is why end-to-end differentiable objectives (Zhang–Zohren–Roberts, AlphaPortfolio-style direct optimisation) and supervised ranking are natural, lower-variance alternatives to policy-gradient RL. RL's credit assignment over time adds value mainly in handling the turnover/cost state.
- A credible design study should include the following arms: (a) a supervised ranker with top-K equal weight; (b) the same ranker with a cost-aware no-trade band; (c) end-to-end differentiable Sharpe or utility training; and (d) RL or GRPO. All should use identical features, costs and splits, run over at least 10 seeds, and be reported with multiplicity-adjusted significance.

### Gaps
- No primary-source head-to-head of RL against LTR top-K equal weight was found, for weekly or any frequency.
- Sun et al.'s RL-for-quant-trading survey (ACM TIST) and the most recent López de Prado or AQR pieces specifically on RL were not retrieved.
- The cost assumptions, universe and test length behind Sood et al.'s Sharpe of 1.17 against 0.68 could not be confirmed. The universe is believed to be S&P 500 sector indices, but I could not verify this from the extract.

## Q3. Pitfalls: non-stationarity, single historical path, low signal-to-noise, seed variance, look-ahead in environments, unrealistic fills and costs, reward hacking, survivorship, and few independent decision points

### Takeaway
The dominant failure modes are statistical rather than algorithmic: a single noisy path, too few samples for RL's sample complexity, seed and hyperparameter variance large enough to manufacture "outperformance", and selection over many trials. Environment-level bugs (same-bar fills, current constituents, frictionless or proportional-only costs) then inflate whatever is left.

### Cited Findings
- **Seed variance and reporting.** Henderson et al., "Deep RL that Matters" (AAAI 2018) [PR]: even averaging several runs across different random seeds "can lead to the reporting of misleading results". Without significance testing and standardised reporting it is "difficult to determine whether improvements… are meaningful". The paper ran five trials per evaluation. — [arXiv 1709.06560](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560)
- **Seed variance in finance.** Sharpe varies substantially across seeds, and outperformance often vanishes under multi-run evaluation and family-wise error correction. — [Unstable Gains, JFDS 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405918826000309). The FinRL contest organisers say policies are "sensitive to hyperparameters, market noise and random seeds", and that this is amplified in crypto. — [Wiley AIE 2025](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/aie2.12004). DeepScalper names seed variance as "a major concern of RL-based trading algorithms". — [arXiv 2201.09058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09058)
- **Sample complexity and low signal-to-noise.** More than 2 million steps, about 8,000 years of daily data, were needed even in a clean synthetic market, and off-policy critics failed because of noisy rewards. — [arXiv 2307.07694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07694). Man Group cites low signal-to-noise as what historically rendered ML techniques useless. — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/the-big-picture-ai)
- **Generalisation and decay.** Most DRL algorithms "degrad[e] quickly during backtesting". — [arXiv 2306.10950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10950). Insufficient data volume leads to low reproducibility. — [AI Review 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-024-11066-w)
- **Backtest overfitting.** Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (*J. Computational Finance* 20(4), 2017) [PR]: combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV) estimates the share of splits in which the in-sample best configuration lands in the bottom half out of sample, so the trial count drives overfitting risk. — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [LuxAlgo explainer](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/probability-of-backtest-overfitting/). A secondary summary of the authors' related work: more than 45 configurations on a 5-year sample almost guarantees an in-sample Sharpe of 1 with an expected out-of-sample Sharpe of 0. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/measuring-investment-strategy-snooping-bias/)
- **Sharpe statistics.** Lo (FAJ 2002) [PR]: Sharpe ratios cannot be annualised by √q except under special conditions. Positive autocorrelation overstates annualised Sharpe and negative autocorrelation understates it. — [CFA Institute FAJ 2002](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2002/the-statistics-of-sharpe-ratios)
- **Look-ahead, survivorship, weak benchmarks, costs and capacity** are the named failure links in the alpha-translation chain, with documented "corrected look-ahead failures". — [arXiv 2609.04917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04917). Survivorship bias is named by the FinRL contest organisers. — [Wiley AIE 2025](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/aie2.12004). Standard fix for same-bar execution: signal on bar t, fill at the first tradable price of bar t+1. — [python.financial](https://python.financial/concepts/look-ahead-bias/). A 2026 paper formalises "look-ahead-freedom as temporal non-interference" as a verifiable property of backtest and agent pipelines (title-level only). — [arXiv 2607.04958](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04958)
- **Slippage and fills.** HRPM's motivation is that prior RL-PM work assumed instant reallocation and ignored slippage, and it treats commission alone as an insufficient cost model. — [AAAI OJS 16142](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16142). Market-impact modelling for RL trading environments is a 2026 topic in its own right. — [arXiv 2603.29086](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29086). A later paper notes PPN's experiments did not fully account for transaction costs. — [arXiv 2201.04699](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04699)
- **Reward shaping and hacking.** A practitioner write-up (opinion) argues that simple-return rewards encourage excessive risk-taking. — [Kinlay blog](https://jonathankinlay.com/2026/03/reinforcement-learning-for-portfolio-optimization-from-theory-to-implementation/). Mean-variance λ that is too large made rewards explode and crashed training, and λ that is too small made rewards near-constant so nothing was learned. — [arXiv 2010.12245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12245)
- **Implausible numbers as a red flag.** One risk-sensitive DRL paper reports Sortino 57.8 and Sharpe 4.34 with an internally inconsistent table. — [ResearchGate 392926739](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392926739_Risk-Sensitive_Deep_Reinforcement_Learning_for_Portfolio_Optimization). A Sharpe above 3 warrants scrutiny. — [Pippas et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10932v2)
- **Short tests in favourable regimes.** EIIE used about 50-day crypto windows. — [arXiv 1706.10059](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10059). FinRL's Dow-30 test covered one year (July 2020 to June 2021). — [FinRL write-up](https://sia.hackernoon.com/finrls-implementation-of-drl-algorithms-for-stock-trading)

### Inferences
- **Few independent decision points at weekly frequency.** Ten years of weekly rebalancing gives about 520 portfolio decisions. Under the IID approximation (Lo 2002), the standard error of an annualised Sharpe is about √((1+SR²/2)/T_years), roughly 0.32 at T = 10 for SR near 0.5–1. Telling apart two strategies whose Sharpe differs by 0.3 is therefore close to impossible on one path. With 30 or more RL configurations and seeds tried, deflation or PBO adjustment is mandatory. (Derived inference; the formula is not quoted from the extract.)
- **Non-stationarity plus one path.** Walk-forward folds (for example 16 folds in Kashif & Ślepaczuk) and multi-market tests are the minimum. Synthetic or bootstrapped market generators can test learnability, as Lu does, but not alpha.
- **Reward hacking in long-only top-K.** Expected hacks: (a) concentration into the most volatile names, if the reward is raw return; (b) zero-turnover "do nothing" or equal-weight collapse, if cost or risk penalties dominate a weak signal (seen in the EIIE stock replication); (c) exploiting stale or adjusted prices around corporate actions; (d) parking in cash during back-tested crashes, which is learned from a handful of episodes such as 2008 and 2020.
- **Survivorship in a 1,000–3,000-name global universe.** Point-in-time constituent lists including delisted names are required. Free datasets that hold only current listings will bias exactly the mid-cap names an RL selector loves, the past winners.
- **Retail cost realism.** The 0–0.65% per-trade range likely mixes FX conversion, stamp duty (for example UK 0.5% on purchases) and spread. These are asymmetric between buys and sells, so the cost model should allow different buy and sell rates, as EIIE and DeepPocket do. With 15 holdings of about £667 each, any fixed minimum commission becomes a large percentage, so a proportional-only cost model understates costs. (Inference about the user's setting, not sourced.)

### Gaps
- No quantified estimate was found of how much look-ahead or same-close execution inflates FinRL-style results. I also could not confirm whether FinRL's default environment fills at the observed close or uses current Dow constituents.
- No source quantified survivorship bias for global mid caps. A figure of 0.1–0.9% a year for S&P 500 current-constituent bias appeared in a search summary, but I could not attribute it to a specific primary source, so it is not used.
- No paper quantified reward hacking (concentration or leverage) for weekly long-only RL.

## Q4. Risk-aware objectives: which work per step versus per episode, typical λ and targets for "modest risk", and exact cost inclusion

### Takeaway
For a weekly long-only book, the most stable choices are the per-step net log return (Kelly) with an explicit variance or downside penalty, and the differential Sharpe ratio, which turns Sharpe into a per-step reward. Sortino, Calmar/drawdown and CVaR are path- or episode-level and need either episode-level policy gradients (GRPO-style group rollouts fit well) or per-step approximations. Costs should enter exactly through the EIIE transaction-remainder factor μ_t applied to drifted weights. I found no RL-specific consensus on λ.

### Cited Findings

**Differential Sharpe ratio (DSR), per step and online [PR]**
- Moody & Saffell (NIPS 1998) and Moody, Wu, Liao & Saffell (1998, *Journal of Forecasting*; one source lists it as *International Journal of Forecasting* 17(5/6):441–470). Performance functions studied: profit or wealth, utility, Sharpe and DSR. For a long/short trader, maximising DSR "yields more consistent results than maximizing profits". For portfolios, DSR-trained traders achieve better risk-adjusted returns than profit-trained ones. — [NIPS 1998](http://papers.neurips.cc/paper/1551-reinforcement-learning-for-trading.pdf); [Performance functions paper record](https://iaorifors.com/paper/27449); [Moody & Saffell 2001, IEEE TNN](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5602298_Learning_to_trade_via_direct_reinforcement)
- Recursion: A_t = A_{t-1} + η(R_t − A_{t-1}) and B_t = B_{t-1} + η(R_t² − B_{t-1}). **D_t = (B_{t-1}ΔA_t − ½A_{t-1}ΔB_t) / (B_{t-1} − A_{t-1}²)^{3/2}**, the first-order effect of R_t on an exponential-moving-average Sharpe with decay η. It is used because standard Sharpe "does not decompose step by step". — [arXiv 2110.04745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04745); [NIPS 1998](http://papers.neurips.cc/paper/1551-reinforcement-learning-for-trading.pdf)
- Limitation: DSR is an approximation, so the learning objective differs from the realised Sharpe. A 2025 paper proposes an exact recursive Sharpe aggregation instead. — [arXiv 2507.08537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08537)
- DSR plus a drawdown-increase penalty scaled by a coefficient has been used as a combined reward. DSR is also the reward in Sood et al.'s DRL-versus-MVO study. — [arXiv 2602.17098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17098)

**Mean–variance utility (return − λ·variance)**
- MVPI (Zhang, Liu & Whiteson, AAAI 2021) [PR] optimises per-step reward variance: E[R] − λ·V(R) = E[R − λ(R − E[R])²] (after Bisi et al.). This reduces to a **modified per-step reward** that standard risk-neutral RL algorithms can use. — [AAAI OJS 17302](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/17302); [arXiv 2004.10888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10888)
- Tamar, Di Castro & Mannor (2012) derived likelihood-ratio policy gradients for mean-variance. — cited in [arXiv 1404.3862](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.3862)
- An exploratory multi-asset MV-RL paper uses risk aversion γ = 1.5 on terminal wealth. — [arXiv 2505.07537](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07537)
- λ sensitivity: too large explodes rewards and crashes training; too small leaves rewards near-constant and nothing is learned. — [arXiv 2010.12245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12245)

**CVaR (episode-level) [PR]**
- Tamar, Glassner & Mannor (AAAI 2015), "Optimizing the CVaR via Sampling": the CVaR gradient written as a conditional expectation, giving a likelihood-ratio (policy-gradient-style) estimator with local-optimum convergence. Applied to Tetris, not portfolios. — [arXiv 1404.3862](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.3862)

**Sortino, downside deviation, Calmar and drawdown**
- Survey definitions: Calmar relates return to maximum drawdown; Sortino penalises only downside deviation. — [arXiv 2212.06064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06064)
- A 2025 risk-aware reward combines return with a Sortino-like downside-volatility term "discouraging asymmetric losses". — [arXiv 2506.04358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04358)
- DeepTrader uses negative maximum drawdown as the reward for its market-scoring unit, which is a path-level quantity. — [AAAI OJS 16144](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16144)

**Log utility / Kelly**
- Log utility is the natural per-step additive objective: Lu's synthetic benchmark uses Kelly (log utility) so the optimal policy is analytic. — [arXiv 2307.07694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07694)
- EIIE's reward is the average log return net of commission. — [arXiv 1706.10059](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10059)
- PPN modifies log-growth with transaction-cost and risk constraints and claims near-optimal growth rate. — [arXiv 2003.03051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03051)

**Volatility scaling and targeting**
- Zhang, Zohren & Roberts, "Deep RL for Trading" (*Journal of Financial Data Science*, Spring 2020) [PR]: volatility scaling built into the reward sizes positions by market volatility. Tested on the 50 most liquid futures, 2011–2019; beats time-series momentum baselines and stays profitable "even with heavy transaction costs". — [arXiv 1911.10107](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10107); [JFDS PDF](https://www.oxford-man.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Deep-Reinforcement-Learning-for-Trading.pdf)
- The end-to-end Sharpe-optimising LSTM studied different "risk levels via volatility scaling". — [arXiv 2005.13665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13665)

**Exact transaction-cost inclusion (EIIE formulation)**
- Weights drift before rebalancing: w'_t = (y_t ⊙ w_{t-1}) / (y_t · w_{t-1}), where y_t is the vector of price relatives. Rebalancing from w'_t to the target w_t shrinks value by the transaction remainder factor μ_t ∈ (0,1]: p_t = μ_t·p'_t. Simple return ρ_t = μ_t·(y_t·w_{t-1}) − 1, and log reward r_t = ln(μ_t·y_t·w_{t-1}). μ_t is the solution of a fixed-point equation in the separate buy rate c_p and sell rate c_s, with ReLU terms on the sell side, derived in the replication's appendix. — [arXiv 1706.10059](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10059); [arXiv 2409.08426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08426); [DeepPocket arXiv 2105.08664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08664)
- Common approximation: μ_t ≈ 1 − c·TO_t, where TO_t = Σ_i|w_{t,i} − w'_{t,i}| and c = 0.25% in that paper. MSPM uses β = 0.0025 with an L1 turnover cost. — [arXiv 2602.01388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01388); [MSPM arXiv 2102.03502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03502)
- Kashif & Ślepaczuk put transaction costs, a separate turnover penalty and diversification constraints in the reward. — [arXiv 2605.17307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17307)

**Empirical reward comparisons**
- No single controlled study compares Sharpe, Sortino, log-return and drawdown rewards on the same data, costs and algorithm. A factor-based DRL study (PLOS ONE) found SAC cut drawdowns at the expense of gains while TD3 had the best cumulative return. A thesis found no significant gain from a goal-oriented reward. — [PLOS ONE 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0332779); [Lund thesis](https://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/record/9178260/file/9178261.pdf)

### Inferences
These are design guidance for a weekly long-only GRPO or transformer agent, derived from the findings above.
- **Per step (dense and additive):** net log return ln(μ_t·y_t·w_{t-1}) (Kelly); a variance penalty in MVPI form, r_t − λ(r_t − r̄)²; a downside penalty, r_t − λ_d·min(r_t − τ, 0)²; DSR; an explicit turnover penalty −κ·TO_t; and a concentration penalty, for example −ζ·Σw_i² (a Herfindahl index). All are differentiable in the weights, given realised returns.
- **Per episode or path only:** Sharpe, Sortino and Calmar over the horizon, maximum drawdown, and CVaR of horizon return. These fit GRPO naturally. Sample G candidate weight paths or rankings for the same start date, roll each over H weeks (for example 4–13), score each path by net Sortino, Calmar or a utility, and use the group-standardised score as the advantage. Because all members of a group face the same realised market, standardising within the group cancels the common market return. The advantage then measures relative selection and sizing skill, which reduces variance. The flip side is that absolute risk level gets no gradient unless the score explicitly penalises volatility, drawdown or beta.
- **Choosing λ ("modest risk").** Under IID returns, mean and variance both scale linearly with horizon, so a mean-variance γ in E[r] − (γ/2)Var(r) is horizon-invariant. Log utility corresponds to γ ≈ 1 (full Kelly, aggressive). Half-Kelly corresponds to γ ≈ 2. Values of about 3–5 are the "moderate investor" range commonly used in asset-allocation work, but I could not source that range in this search. Example: with 18% annual volatility, γ = 4 charges 4/2 × 0.0324 ≈ 6.5% a year, comparable to the equity premium. That is a strong penalty that will push towards low-volatility names or equal weight. Calibrate λ so that the in-sample optimiser's realised volatility hits a target, for example 10–15% a year, about 0.6–0.8 of a global equity index's volatility, rather than fixing λ a priori.
- **Volatility targeting as a constraint rather than a reward.** Scale gross exposure to cash so that predicted portfolio volatility is at most the target (for example 12% annualised). This is simpler and more stable than learning risk aversion through the reward, and it mirrors the reward volatility-scaling approach of Zhang, Zohren & Roberts.
- **Cost arithmetic for the user's range.** Per-week cost is about c·TO_t. Replacing 3 of 15 equal-weight names (TO ≈ 0.4) at c = 0.65% costs about 0.26% a week, roughly 13.5% a year. At c = 0.10% the same turnover costs about 2.1% a year. Therefore (i) use exact μ_t, or c·TO_t with separate buy and sell rates, in both training and evaluation; (ii) train across a randomised cost range (for example c ∈ [0, 0.65%]) or condition the policy on c; and (iii) add a no-trade band or hold-unless-rank-drops-below-K' rule as a structural turnover control. Evidence that RL learns low turnover on its own is weak; the EIIE stock replication collapsed to static equal weight rather than learning to trade selectively.

### Gaps
- No source gave a recommended λ, γ or volatility target specifically for RL portfolio rewards beyond the single γ = 1.5 example. The 3–5 "moderate" range above is unsourced background knowledge.
- I could not verify from primary text whether Moody & Saffell (2001) define a differential downside deviation ratio.
- No head-to-head study of DSR against log-utility, Sortino-shaped, CVaR or drawdown rewards on equities with costs was found.
- No portfolio application of the Tamar CVaR gradient with out-of-sample equity results was found.

## Q5. Constraints that matter in practice (long-only, max weight, max positions, sector/country caps, cash) and how RL papers enforce them

### Takeaway
Most RL portfolio papers enforce only long-only plus budget, via a softmax over assets plus cash. Per-name caps, cardinality and sector caps are rare. When present, they are imposed by projection or safety layers, Dirichlet policies, or action-space decomposition. Hard projections can starve gradients, and softmax-then-project couples exploration across assets.

### Cited Findings
- **Softmax with a cash asset (EIIE family):** long-only, sum-to-one, with BTC or cash as an explicit asset. — [arXiv 1706.10059](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10059). Softmax guarantees non-negative weights that sum to 1 but has intrinsic limits for caps and masks. — [Attention-RL arXiv 2510.06466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06466)
- **Dirichlet policy on the simplex:** "enforces feasibility by construction and accommodates tradability masks". Per-name caps are applied optionally by capped-simplex projection. — [arXiv 2510.06466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06466). Hierarchical Dirichlet policies (for example region, then stock) with diversification constraints. — [arXiv 2605.17307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17307)
- **Safety layer for cardinality and bounds (Li et al., IJCNN 2024) [PR]:** "standard DRL… struggles to incorporate… cardinality and bounding"; any infeasible action is mapped to the feasible region by a safety layer that encodes an extended Markowitz model, trained with projection-based interior-point policy optimisation. — [Univ. Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/cardinality-and-bounding-constrained-portfolio-optimization-using/)
- **Projection pitfalls:** the softmax-then-project pattern "couples exploration across coordinates and introduces projection-induced discontinuities". Hard projections can block gradients ("vanishing gradients at the constraint boundaries"). A soft-radial projection beat orthogonal projection, DC3 and HardNet on a capped simplex with per-asset capacity. — [arXiv 2602.03461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03461)
- **Action-space decomposition** for constrained portfolio management (Springer, 2023 conference chapter). — [Springer 978-3-031-33377-4_29](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-33377-4_29)
- **Long-short designs** (DeepTrader's long/short fund split, AlphaStock and AlphaPortfolio winner-loser books) do not enforce long-only at all. — [AAAI OJS 16144](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16144); [arXiv 1908.02646](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02646); [NBER w35195](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35195)
- **Rank-then-optimise pipelines** (FinRankGRPO) move constraint handling into a downstream mean-variance optimiser. — [arXiv 2609.24175](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24175v1)

### Inferences
These are a practical recipe for 10–20 names, long-only, from 1,000–3,000 stocks.
- **Separate selection from sizing.** The policy (transformer) outputs scores over a tradable-masked universe. A deterministic top-K step (K = 10–20) gives cardinality exactly. Weights within the selected set come from a capped softmax or capped-simplex projection: for example w_i ≤ 10%, an optional w_i ≥ 3% floor to avoid uneconomic tiny positions at £10k, and cash as an extra "asset" or residual.
- **Sector and country caps** can be enforced in the same projection, as linear constraints solved by a small QP or iterative capping. They then apply identically in training rollouts and in live use.
- **For GRPO, sample at the selection level.** Use a Plackett–Luce or Gumbel-top-K sample over scores to give G candidate portfolios. The log-probability of a sampled top-K set is tractable under Plackett–Luce, so the policy gradient stays well-defined. Weight caps are a deterministic post-processing step that the reward sees, and turnover is computed against the drifted current holdings.
- **Minimum trade size and integer shares** matter at £10k with 15 names (about £667 each). Round in the environment and charge any fixed fees there. Otherwise the agent will learn to make many tiny rebalances that are free in simulation and costly in practice.

### Gaps
- No RL portfolio paper was found that enforces sector or country caps and reports their out-of-sample effect.
- Out-of-sample performance numbers for the IJCNN 2024 safety-layer method were not visible.
- No study compared a Plackett–Luce or top-K policy parameterisation against softmax or Dirichlet for portfolio RL.
